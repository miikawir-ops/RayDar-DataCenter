FINANCING · CONTENT REVIEWED 9/2026

Financing models

How data centers and GPU capacity are financed, and who carries the debt
KEY IDEA
Financing rests on the customer's commitment and creditworthiness, not on the property alone.
A long lease or take-or-pay contract makes future cash flow predictable, and the customer's creditworthiness decides how cheaply you can borrow against it.

REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES
Meta and Oracle (2025)
CORPORATE DEBT
In October 2025 Meta issued $30 billion of bonds, the largest investment grade issue since 2023. Oracle raised $18 billion in September, and banks arranged about $38 billion of loans for data centers that Oracle will use.
Meta Hyperion, Louisiana (2025)
OFF-BALANCE-SHEET JOINT VENTURE
Blue Owl's funds own 80% and Meta 20% of the joint venture building the campus; a separate entity of the venture issued about $27 billion of bonds. Meta is the tenant and gives a 16-year residual value guarantee.
Aligned and AirTrunk (2024–2026)
INFRASTRUCTURE, PENSION AND SOVEREIGN CAPITAL
BlackRock's GIP, Abu Dhabi's MGX and the AIP partnership (including Microsoft and Nvidia) bought Aligned from Macquarie's infrastructure funds at a valuation of about $40 billion (2026). Blackstone and the Canadian pension fund CPP Investments bought AirTrunk for about $16 billion (2024).
Vantage Data Centers (2024–2026)
DATA CENTER ABS
Vantage carried out the first data center ABS deals in North America and Europe. For example, notes backed by the leases of its Wales campus were issued for £600 million (2024) and another £254 million (2026).
CoreWeave (2023–2026)
GPU-BACKED LOANS
The first $2.3 billion loan (2023) put the GPUs and the customer contracts in the same SPV. In March 2026 an $8.5 billion loan became the first GPU financing rated investment grade, because it was secured by a contract with Meta.
Nebius (2025)
CONVERTIBLE NOTES AND A SHARE ISSUE
After the Microsoft contract, Nebius raised about $3.75 billion through convertible notes and a share issue to buy land and computing capacity. The company said it would also finance construction with debt raised against the Microsoft contract.
Equinix (2020–2025)
GREEN BONDS
Equinix has issued about $9 billion of green bonds. The proceeds are earmarked for energy-efficient sites: in Europe a new data center's PUE must be at most 1.35. The Paris PA10 data center, for example, was financed this way.
xAI Colossus 2, Memphis (2025)
GPU LEASING AND MANUFACTURER FINANCING
According to news reports, an SPV of about $20 billion buys Nvidia GPUs and leases them to xAI for five years. Nvidia takes part with equity: the equipment supplier finances its customer.

RISKS FROM THE LENDER'S POINT OF VIEW
Customer risk
The price of a loan often follows the customer's creditworthiness more than the property or the equipment itself. CoreWeave's loans are cheaper when the customer is rated investment grade.
Duration mismatch
Leases and ABS can run 15–20 years, but GPU equipment becomes obsolete in a few years. GPU loans are therefore shorter and amortize quickly.
Residual value risk
Who bears the risk if the asset or the equipment is worth less when the contract ends? A residual value guarantee moves the risk back to the tenant.
Construction risk
There is no cash flow before completion. Construction financing is more expensive, and it is refinanced once the site is finished and leased.
Interest rate and refinancing risk
Debt often has to be refinanced partway through an asset's life. Vantage's ABS notes, for example, have an expected repayment in five years but a final maturity only in 30 years. The interest rate at the time of refinancing decides the cost of the financing.
Guarantee or non-recourse
Who answers if the cash flow falls short? CoreWeave's $8.5 billion loan was non-recourse, because it was secured by an investment grade customer's contract. CoreWeave itself guaranteed a $3.1 billion loan taken against contracts with lower-rated customers.
TERMS ON THIS PAGE
SPV
special purpose vehicle
a separate company set up to own one asset and carry its debt
Project finance
a loan repaid from the asset's own cash flow, not from the owner's balance sheet
Non-recourse
the lender can recover its claim only from the asset and its cash flow, not from the owner company
ABS
asset-backed securities
notes secured by, e.g., lease receivables
DDTL
delayed draw term loan
a loan drawn in installments as equipment is bought
Investment grade
a good credit rating (BBB- or better), which lowers the price of a loan
Residual value guarantee
the tenant guarantees the asset's value if it doesn't renew the contract
Off-balance sheet
the debt sits on another company's balance sheet, even though the benefit and part of the risk are the user's
Vendor financing
an equipment supplier finances its customer's purchases, e.g. by investing in it
Refinancing
an expensive construction loan is replaced with a cheaper one once the site is finished
Bond
a loan issued by a company, which investors buy and which can be traded
Convertible note
a bond that can later be converted into the company's shares
Infrastructure fund
a fund that invests in long-lived assets with steady cash flow
Parent company guarantee
the parent company answers for a subsidiary's or SPV's debt if it can't pay
Green bond
a loan whose proceeds are earmarked for environmental projects, e.g. an energy-efficient data center
Sustainability-linked loan
a loan whose interest rate depends on meeting agreed sustainability targets

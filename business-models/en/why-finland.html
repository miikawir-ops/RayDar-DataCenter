Why Finland?

Why data centers, like those of Google, Nebius and Verda, are built in Finland
ELECTRICITY

Clean, competitive electricity

Much of the production is carbon-free nuclear, hydro and wind power. Data centers can sign long power purchase agreements for clean electricity. According to Eurostat, business electricity was the cheapest in the EU in Finland in the second half (H2) of 2025 (7.48 c/kWh vs. an EU average of 18.37 c/kWh, consumption band 500–2,000 MWh).

GRID

A reliable transmission grid

The reliability of Fingrid's transmission grid (kantaverkko) was 99.99995% in 2025. The whole country is one electricity price area.

TIME-TO-MARKET

Fast grid connections

According to Fingrid, connections can be built quickly by international standards, especially in Northern Finland. Power is the industry's scarcest resource, so speed is a competitive advantage.

COOLING

A cool climate

Outside air can do the cooling for much of the year. The PUE of Nebius's Mäntsälä data center, for example, is about 1.1.

WASTE HEAT

Extensive district heating networks

The servers' heat can be sold to the district heating (kaukolämpö) network. In Mäntsälä, heat has been sold to the district heating network since 2015.

GROUND

Stable bedrock

A very low earthquake risk and few natural disaster risks, which reduces the risks of construction and operation.

SPACE

Land and industrial sites

Room for large campuses, often next to existing power infrastructure: Google's Hamina data center is a former paper mill.

CONNECTIVITY

Telecom connections

A direct submarine cable to Central Europe (C-Lion1 to Germany) and good connections to the Nordic countries.

SOCIETY

A stable operating environment

EU membership, the rule of law and data protection regulation support European data sovereignty.

SKILLS

Energy and ICT skills

Strong skills in electrical engineering, automation and ICT. Training for the industry has also been started, for example as a collaboration between Google and EKAMI in Hamina.

HEADWINDS
The electricity tax went up

On 1 July 2026 data centers moved from electricity tax class II to class I: the tax rose from 0.05 cents to 2.24 cents per kilowatt hour.

Building isn't cheaper

In Turner & Townsend's 2026 index, the cost of building a data center in Helsinki is $13.33/W. Helsinki, Stockholm, Oslo and Copenhagen are all among the index's ten most expensive.

Winter price risk

The average price is low, but it swings widely: in the first nine weeks of 2026, electricity in Finland was on average more expensive than in Germany.

Connection capacity is getting scarce

Enquiries for consumption connections to the transmission grid exceed 100 GW, more than half of them from data centers. Fingrid is preparing rules for allocating the capacity.

The replacement aid was dropped

In December 2025 the Government outlined a ten-year tax-relief aid with a tax revenue loss of at most €30 million. It was meant to replace data centers' lower electricity tax, which ended in June, but the Government dropped the aid in September 2026 in the budget negotiations for 2027.

Public debate

The adequacy of electricity supply, the price effects and land use raise questions locally.

As of October 2026. Sources: Fingrid (half-year report, 23 July 2026, in Finnish), Eurostat, Turner & Townsend, Finnish Government (5 December 2025, in Finnish), Uusi Suomi (September 2026, in Finnish), The Star / Xinhua (2 September 2026), company publications and industry news.

Retail colocation

Colocation at retail scale: AI Ltd rents data center space for its own servers
SITUATION

AI Ltd has used a GPU cloud for a long time, and its use is continuous. The monthly bill keeps growing, and its own servers would be cheaper in the long run.

DECISION

AI Ltd buys its own GPU servers and rents rack space for them in a colocation operator's data center.

REAL-WORLD COMPARISON

The GPU cloud Nebius places some of its own servers in Equinix's Paris data center, which makes it a colocation customer itself.

CONTRACT

Colocation agreement

WHAT AI LTD BUYS
Space and services: a mix of lease and service. The servers are AI Ltd's own.
PARTIES
AI Ltd (customer) and the colocation operator
CONTRACT STRUCTURE
An MSA (framework agreement) and separate orders (order forms) for space, power and connections
WHAT IS DELIVERED
Rack space or a cage, reserved power (kW), cooling, physical security and access to telecom carriers' networks
WHAT IS PAID FOR
Monthly fees (MRC): space per rack, power (kW or kWh), cross-connects per unit. Plus one-off installation fees (NRC) and remote hands work by the hour
TERM
Typically 1–5 years, often renewing automatically
SLA
Availability of power and cooling, typically 99.99–99.999%. Credits for shortfalls (service credits)
NOT INCLUDED
The servers, software or data, which are AI Ltd's

NETWORK ECOSYSTEM

In retail colocation, AI Ltd gets more than rack space: it gets access to the telecom carriers and cloud services in the same building. It can connect to them directly with a cross-connect, a cable inside the building, so the traffic doesn't go over the public internet. A direct connection is fast, reliable and inexpensive. The more parties in the building, the more valuable it is to each of them: Equinix has over 10,500 customers, more than 2,000 of them network service providers (2025).

TERMS ON THIS CARD
Rack
a standard-size equipment cabinet; the smallest rental unit
Cage and suite
cage = a fenced area in the data hall, suite = a separate closed room; each for one customer
White space
the data hall where the customers' racks are
Grey space
the technical rooms for the power and cooling systems
Demarc
demarcation point
the responsibility boundary: the point where the operator's responsibility ends and the customer's begins
MSA
master service agreement
a framework agreement under which orders are placed
MRC and NRC
monthly / non-recurring charge
a monthly fee and a one-off fee
Cross-connect
a direct cable connection between two parties in a data center
SLA and service credit
a promised service level, and a credit if the promise isn't met
kW and kWh
kW is the reserved power, how much electricity the equipment can draw at once; kWh is the amount of energy used. In retail colocation the price is often based on reserved power (kW), and the energy used (kWh) can be billed separately.
AI Ltd is fictional; the other companies are real. The contract terms are typical features of the industry, not any one operator's terms. Remote hands = equipment work done by the operator's staff.

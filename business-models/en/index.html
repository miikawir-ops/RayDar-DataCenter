THE LAYERS OF A DATA CENTER

What a company handles in-house and what it buys

Every model contains the whole data center, from the property to the applications. The models differ in how much of it the company handles in-house and how much it buys from a counterparty.

As an analogy, the data center is a factory and the servers are its production machines: in the cloud you buy the factory's output, in colocation you put your own machines in someone else's factory, and with your own data center the whole factory is yours.

The line depends on the service bought: in IaaS the company manages the operating system; in PaaS and SaaS the counterparty handles more (shared responsibility model).

The layers don't show every difference.

  • Public cloud and GPU cloud: the difference is above all the commitment. In a GPU cloud, capacity is often reserved for your own use with a multi-year contract.
  • Retail and wholesale: the difference is scale and the contract. Wholesale is a whole data hall or building on a 5–15-year lease.
  • Retail colocation also gains value from its network ecosystem: the carriers and cloud services in the same building, which customers can connect to directly (cross-connect). A wholesale customer cares more about power capacity and cooling.
  • In the cloud models the responsibility line also depends on the service bought.

SPECIALIZED MODELS

Other models in the industry

Cloud giants' leased capacity: AWS, Microsoft and Google lease part of their capacity from colocation companies and GPU clouds. The largest source of wholesale demand.
Interconnection: revenue comes from connections between networks, not only from space (e.g. Equinix, FICIX).
Edge data centers: small data centers close to users, for low latency.
Development and financing models: the property can be owned by a fund, a REIT or a developer that leases it to an operator (e.g. build-to-suit, sale-leaseback).
Waste heat: the data center sells its heat to the district heating network (e.g. Nebius in Mäntsälä).
FIVE THINGS TO REMEMBER
1
Lease or service? The models differ in what AI Ltd buys: the right to use space, or ready capacity.
2
Operating is not owning. The building can belong to an investor, the equipment to AI Ltd and the operation to a third party.
3
A lower price takes more risk. E.g. a GPU cloud customer pays for reserved capacity even if it doesn't use it.
4
Models chain together. Several companies in different roles and different countries can be behind the same service.
5
Financing rests on the customer's commitment and creditworthiness, not on the property alone. A long contract makes the cash flow predictable, and creditworthiness decides the price of the loan.
Terms: public cloud = a commercial cloud service shared by many customers · colocation = rented data center space for your own equipment · retail = racks or small spaces for many customers · wholesale = a whole data hall or building for one tenant · rack = equipment cabinet · cross-connect = a direct cable connection between two parties · remote hands = equipment work done by the operator's staff · take-or-pay = payment regardless of use · utilization = who pays if reserved capacity goes unused; the risk of unleased or unsold capacity sits with the counterparty (the CSP, the GPU cloud or the operator).
